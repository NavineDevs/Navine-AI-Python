Just like how adding extra nutrients when it's got all the nutrients it needs already won't make any difference. Yeah, because if a plant has a single rootstock it can only process so much nutrients. Increase the scale of its root network and it can increase its processing of nutrients, especially when there are two or more main stems fusing into one. Plants with a tri-rootstock networks have been shown in preliminary studies ro have overall increase in diameter of around 150%, an increase in total leaf surface area of 80% and an increased leaf count of 50%. The only downside shown is a minor decrease to the plants height compared to singular rootstock of about -10%. Then there's the histlrical practice of orchard grafting where tree grafters sometimes graft together multiple saplings of the same variety to improve their resilience to harsh weather conditions.
