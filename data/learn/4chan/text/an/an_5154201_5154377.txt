>>5154269 I'm no expert but it looks like it has grown already quite tall, but honestly, it entirely depends on the style of bonsai you're going with. I'd remove the first three lower stems from the main stem with a clean blade to make it seem neater, then I would suggest you either trim down the two branch cannopies horizontally that have split from the main stems upper node so you have an even cannopy with some leaves still present, or alternatively, cut one of the branches off wholesale and keep the other. If you choose the last option I'd then look into bonsai bending to curve the remaining branch overtime very slowly at an 8 or 4 o clock angle (some use wire, string or curved plastic with a channel to fit the branch in to do it). Or you can do the autistic nuclear option and tanuki it. Get a hunk of dried drift or bogwood and cut a channel along it and then align the ficus's main steam along the channel with some bonsai wire. Over time the main stem will fuse with the bog or driftwood. Pic rel is a very simple and basic example of a tanuki and with your bonsai I think that might be something worth looking at due to how tall it is already. You can also drill out a hole at the base of the dried wood with a diameter wide enough to feed the main stem of the ficus through and then bend it along the length of the dried wood, finishing it off by covering the base of the dried wood with substrate so it looks like the ficus is an actual branch growing from the dried wood. That though is more finnicky to pull off though as you need to feed the main steam AND it's cannopy through the drilled hole, which is doable, but you run the risk of damaging the cannopy in the process.
