>>1528673 (Cont) A White House official told the Journal that the United States had already achieved its military objectives against Iran. The administration has publicly maintained that its operations substantially degraded Iran’s military and nuclear capabilities, although continued Iranian attacks and the disruption of shipping through Hormuz have complicated that claim. According to the report, Trump’s immediate focus is now the strait. The president is said to be keeping military options available if Iran continues attacking shipping, even as Washington searches for a diplomatic way to restore maritime traffic. Iran has meanwhile presented a series of far-reaching demands, including a permanent end to the U.S. military campaign, removal of sanctions and the naval blockade, withdrawal of American forces and financial compensation. U.S. officials view several of those demands as nonstarters. The standoff follows earlier attempts by regional mediators to craft a mechanism for reopening Hormuz. Previous proposals included new shipping lanes through Iranian and Omani waters, while Washington has opposed Iranian efforts to impose fees or exercise control over international traffic through the strategic waterway. The Journal reported that Trump has told senior advisers in recent weeks that he believes Tehran will be unable to rebuild its nuclear program, at least during the remainder of his presidency, because of the extensive damage inflicted on Iranian nuclear facilities. Trump reportedly believes U.S. intelligence capabilities would detect attempts by Tehran either to restore activity at damaged sites or secretly develop a nuclear weapon, while the threat of renewed American military action could provide sufficient deterrence.
