The initiative was submitted by citizens rather than the county government — a distinction that could make a major difference at the polls. Under current California court rulings, a citizen-sponsored special tax can pass with a simple majority, while a special tax placed on the ballot by a local government generally requires two-thirds approval. That timing is especially significant because Proposition 43 will also go before California voters this November. If approved, Proposition 43 would require citizen-sponsored special taxes to clear the same two-thirds threshold beginning in 2027.
